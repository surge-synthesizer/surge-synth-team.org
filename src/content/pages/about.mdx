The Surge Synth Team maintains a family of free and open-source audio plugins — synthesizers, samplers and effects. Everything we make is free, and the source is open for everyone.

There's no application process to [join us](/contact). Just show up, be friendly, and contribute. We work hard to make a welcoming community of music enthusiasts, and we really don't have any rules other than "don't be a jerk"! Have an idea? We'd love to hear it! Happen to be a Windows C++ plugin developer? We need your help! Want to contribute content? Go for it!

## How we started

Surge began in 2005 as a commercial synthesizer, written by Claes Johanson ([@kurasu](https://github.com/kurasu)) and sold through his company, Vember Audio. It was one of his first large projects.

By 2018, with [other work](https://www.bitwig.com/) taking his time and little appetite left for maintaining it across platforms, Claes decided to give it a new life and opened the source code. The codebase came from an unfinished 1.6 release, already an improvement on the released 1.5.3 in a number of ways.

What the community built from there became Surge XT, and the team that gathered around it became the Surge Synth Team. Open source brought Surge to new heights and garnered critical acclaim as one of the best free plugins money cannot buy — and since then we've taken on OB-Xf, Shortcircuit XT, SpectrumWorx, Stochas and more!

## Open sourcing your freeware

You made an awesome plugin, and you were cool enough to share it for free. Maybe your life has gone in another direction, and you don't have time to keep working on it. We are an open source community that would love to carry your work forward into the future!

If you would like to know more about open sourcing your audio plugins, please [reach out](/contact)! We might very well be able to help maintain them if you decide to open source them.

We generally work with the GPL3 license for plugins and the MIT license for libraries. If you aren't familiar with open source licenses, we are happy to talk with you about how they work and what it would mean to adopt one. In some cases it's a great idea, but it may not be the right choice for everything.

## CLAP

[CLAP](https://cleveraudio.org/1-feature-overview/) is an open standard for plugins and hosts — fully open source, industry supported, collaboratively developed. We were part of the cross-industry team that built it, and the Surge family of products supported it from launch day. Surge XT was among the first synths to take full advantage of newer features such as polyphonic modulation.

For developers, we help maintain the [CLAP JUCE extensions](https://github.com/free-audio/clap-juce-extensions), the [`clap-info`](https://github.com/surge-synthesizer/clap-info) command line tool, and [`clap-saw-demo`](https://github.com/surge-synthesizer/clap-saw-demo), a tiny synth that demonstrates polyphonic modulation, note expressions and more.
